Michele Oka Doner

Artist, author, and public sculptor whose practice spans eight decades of sustained engagement with the natural world.

Biography

Michele Oka Doner is an internationally renowned artist and author whose work spans five decades. Her artwork is fueled by a lifelong study and appreciation of the natural world, from which she derives her formal vocabulary. Her artistic production encompasses sculpture, public art, prints, drawings, functional objects, artist books, costume and set design, video and other media. She is well known for creating over 40 public and private permanent art installations, including A Walk On The Beach, the mile and a quarter long bronze and terrazzo concourse at Miami International Airport, seen by 40,000,000 travelers a year.

Born in 1945 in Miami Beach, Florida, Oka Doner grew up in a family deeply embedded in the civic and cultural life of South Florida. Her father, Sherwood Oka, served as Mayor of Miami Beach. The natural environment of her childhood — coral reefs, mangrove shores, the tidal margins of Biscayne Bay — became a foundational and lifelong vocabulary.

She received her B.F.A. from the University of Michigan in 1966 and her M.F.A. from Cranbrook Academy of Art in 1968. Through the 1970s Oka Doner worked primarily in Detroit, exhibiting at the Gertrude Kasle Gallery and developing a sustained relationship with the Detroit Institute of Arts. Her landmark 1979 exhibition Image & Object, shown at the DIA and then at PS1 in New York, established her national reputation.

The 1980s brought the first major public commissions, including Celestial Plaza for the American Museum of Natural History, New York (1986). The signature work of the 1990s is A Walk on the Beach (1995), the permanent bronze installation at Miami International Airport — over one mile in length, comprising more than 9,000 individual cast forms.

Subsequent decades brought further major public commissions — including Talisman for Park Avenue, New York (2019) and Palm Goddess for Malta, Valletta (2022). Her books include Natural Order (Assouline, 2005), Welcome to the Land of Symbol, and Knocking on Heaven's Door. She lives and works in New York.

Oka Doner's work is held in the Metropolitan Museum of Art, Whitney Museum of American Art, Chicago Art Institute, Cooper-Hewitt National Design Museum, the Louvre, Victoria & Albert Museum, and collections at Harvard, Princeton, Yale, and Oxford, among numerous others nationally and internationally.
